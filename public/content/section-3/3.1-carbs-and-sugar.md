To keep your blood sugar balanced after meals, you must calculate and inject the correct dose of fast-acting (**bolus**) insulin based on the carbohydrates in your food.

---

### What is Carb Counting?

**Carbohydrate counting** (or "carb counting") is a technique used to match the amount of insulin you inject to the amount of carbohydrates you eat.

Carbohydrates are the primary nutrient in food that turns directly into glucose (sugar) in your bloodstream. Carbs are found in:
* Starchy foods (bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, cereals)
* Sugary foods and drinks (sweets, cakes, fruit juice, sodas)
* Dairy products (milk, yoghurt)
* Fruits and some vegetables

Protein and fat have a minimal immediate impact on blood sugar, so carb counting focuses almost entirely on total carbohydrate grams.

---

### What is an Insulin-to-Carb Ratio (ICR)?

Your **Insulin-to-Carb Ratio (ICR)** tells you how many grams of carbohydrate one unit of fast-acting insulin will cover. 

Because everyone's body responds differently to insulin, your healthcare team helps you determine your personal ratio.

* **Example Ratio:** 1:10 (1 unit of insulin for every 10 grams of carbs)
* **How it works:**
  * If a meal contains **60g of carbs**, you divide 60 by 10.
  * **Calculation:** 60 ÷ 10 = **6 units of bolus insulin**.

---

### How to Count Carbs in a Meal

There are three main steps to calculating carbs accurately:

1. **Read Nutrition Labels:** Look at the "Total Carbohydrate" value on food packaging (not just the "of which sugars" line). Note the serving size.
2. **Weigh or Measure Food:** Use a digital kitchen scale or measuring cups to determine your exact portion size.
3. **Use Reference Guides or Apps:** For unpackaged foods (like fresh fruit or restaurant meals), use carb-counting reference books, databases, or smartphone apps (e.g., Carbs & Cals).

---

### Step-by-Step: Injecting for a Meal

Diabetics follow this process when preparing to eat a meal:

1. **Check Your Blood Sugar:** Measure your current glucose level using a meter or continuous glucose monitor (CGM).
2. **Calculate Meal Insulin:** Count the total grams of carbs in your meal and divide by your Insulin-to-Carb Ratio.
3. **Add or Subtract Correction Insulin (if needed):** If your starting blood sugar is above target, add a correction dose (using your Correction Factor). If it is below target, reduce your dose or treat the low sugar first.
4. **Time Your Injection (Pre-bolus):** Inject fast-acting insulin **15 to 20 minutes before eating**. Pre-bolusing gives the insulin time to start working just as the food begins digesting, preventing post-meal sugar spikes.
5. **Inject Safely:** Inject into a clean site with suitable subcutaneous fat (such as the abdomen or thigh) and rotate injection sites regularly to prevent lipohypertrophy (lumpy tissue under the skin).
